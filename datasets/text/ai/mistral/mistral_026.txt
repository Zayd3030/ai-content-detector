The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors:

1. Economic Crisis: France's economy was in a state of crisis due to heavy government spending, agricultural issues, and the country's involvement in the American Revolutionary War. The burden of debt fell heavily on the common people, leading to widespread poverty and discontent.

2. Social Inequality: France had a rigid feudal system where the nobility and clergy enjoyed immense privileges while the majority of the population lived in abject poverty. This social inequality was a major source of tension and resentment among the common people.

3. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, particularly those of thinkers like Voltaire, Rousseau, and Montesquieu, influenced many French citizens. These ideas emphasized liberty, equality, and the rule of law, which contrasted sharply with the existing feudal system.

4. Absolutism: The absolute rule of King Louis XVI was another significant factor. His unpopular decisions, such as calling the Estates-General in 1789 (a move he later regretted), and his perceived indifference to the plight of the common people, fueled public discontent.

5. Food Shortages: The poor harvests between 1784 and 1788 led to food shortages and skyrocketing prices, exacerbating poverty and social unrest. This situation culminated in the infamous bread riots in Paris.

6. Political Crisis: The political crisis was triggered by King Louis XVI's financial troubles. He attempted to address these issues by calling the Estates-General, a move that set off a chain of events leading to the Revolution.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm of discontent, ultimately leading to the French Revolution and the overthrow of the Bourbon monarchy.
