The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors:

1. Economic Crisis: France's economy was in a state of crisis due to heavy government debt, agricultural problems, and the outdated feudal system. The burden of taxation fell heavily on the common people, while the nobility and clergy were exempt from many taxes.

2. Political Corruption and Absolutism: The French monarchy, particularly under King Louis XVI, was seen as corrupt and ineffective. The monarch's absolute power, combined with political corruption, led to a lack of representation for the common people.

3. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, such as liberty, equality, and the social contract, greatly influenced the French Revolution. These ideas challenged the traditional feudal system and the absolute power of the monarchy.

4. Bourgeoisie Rise: The rise of a new middle class (the bourgeoisie) created a group with enough wealth and education to demand political power. They were frustrated by their lack of representation in the Estates-General, the French legislative body.

5. Food Shortages and Riots: Frequent food shortages and high prices led to bread riots in Paris, most notably the Women's March on Versailles in 1789. These events forced the king to return to Paris and convene the Estates-General.

6. The Estates-General and the National Assembly: The meeting of the Estates-General in May 1789 led to the formation of the National Assembly, which was more representative of the common people. This body declared itself the only legitimate legislative authority in France, marking a significant turning point in the Revolution.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm that led to the French Revolution, ultimately resulting in the fall of the Bourbon monarchy and the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte.
