The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors that contributed to its outbreak:

1. Economic Crisis: France's economy was struggling due to heavy government debt, inflation, and agricultural issues like the decline of feudalism and the Little Ice Age. The burden of taxes fell disproportionately on the common people, leading to widespread poverty and unrest.

2. Social Inequality: The French society was deeply divided between the privileged nobility, clergy, and bourgeoisie, and the impoverished peasantry and urban working class. This social inequality fueled resentment and calls for reform.

3. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, particularly those advocating for liberty, equality, and democracy, had a significant impact on the French people. These ideas challenged the absolute power of the monarchy and the Church, and inspired many to demand political change.

4. Bourbon Absolutism: The reigns of Louis XIV, Louis XV, and especially Louis XVI were marked by increasing centralization of power and disregard for the Estates-General, the representative assembly of the realm. This led to a growing rift between the monarchy and the people.

5. International Factors: France's involvement in the American Revolutionary War and subsequent war debts added to the country's financial woes. Additionally, the influence of neighboring revolutionary movements, such as those in America and the Dutch Batavian Republic, inspired French revolutionaries.

6. Food Shortages and Riots: The period from 1788 to 1789 saw a series of food shortages and price increases, leading to bread riots in Paris. These events further exacerbated social tensions and pushed the common people towards revolution.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm that led to the French Revolution, ultimately resulting in the fall of the Bourbon monarchy, the Reign of Terror, and the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte.
