The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors that contributed to its outbreak:

1. Economic Crisis: France faced severe economic problems, including high inflation, unemployment, and food shortages due to poor harvests. The nobility and the clergy, who were exempt from taxes, further strained the economy.

2. Fiscal Crisis: The French monarchy had accumulated enormous debts from the Seven Years' War and the American Revolution. King Louis XVI attempted to address this issue by calling the Estates-General in 1789, a move that ultimately led to the revolution.

3. Political Dissatisfaction: The French political system was outdated and unrepresentative. Only the First Estate (clergy) and the Third Estate (commoners), who represented over 98% of the population, were dissatisfied with their status.

4. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, particularly those of philosophers like Voltaire, Rousseau, and Montesquieu, influenced many French citizens. These ideas emphasized liberty, equality, and the rule of law, which contrasted sharply with the absolute monarchy in France.

5. Social Unrest: Growing discontent among the common people, peasants, and urban workers led to widespread unrest. The Tennis Court Oath, where representatives from the Third Estate pledged to establish a constitution for France, marked the beginning of organized resistance against the monarchy.

6. International Influence: The success of the American Revolution inspired many French citizens to demand similar reforms. Additionally, foreign powers, such as Austria and Prussia, threatened France's territorial integrity, further fueling dissent.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm that led to the French Revolution, ultimately resulting in the fall of the Bourbon monarchy, the Reign of Terror, and the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte.
