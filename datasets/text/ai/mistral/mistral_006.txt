The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors:

1. Economic Crisis: France's economy was in dire straits due to heavy government spending, agricultural problems, and the country's involvement in the American Revolutionary War. The burden of debt and taxes fell heavily on the common people, leading to widespread discontent.

2. Social Inequality: France had a rigid feudal system where the nobility and clergy enjoyed immense privileges while the majority of the population lived in poverty. This social inequality was a major source of frustration for many.

3. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, particularly those of thinkers like Voltaire, Rousseau, and Montesquieu, influenced many French citizens. These ideas promoted liberty, equality, and democracy, which contrasted sharply with the existing feudal system.

4. Absolutism and Corruption: The monarchy under King Louis XVI was seen as corrupt and out of touch with the people. The king's attempts to raise taxes through the Estates-General (a representative assembly) without giving more power to the third estate (representing the common people) further fueled resentment.

5. Food Shortages: Poor harvests in the late 1780s led to food shortages and high prices, exacerbating social tensions. The bread riots in Paris in 1789 were a significant catalyst for the revolution.

6. International Influence: The influence of foreign powers, particularly Britain and Austria, on French politics also played a role. France's defeat in the Seven Years' War (1756-1763) had left it with territorial losses and a large debt, which contributed to its economic problems.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm of discontent that eventually led to the French Revolution. The revolution would have far-reaching effects not only on France but also on Europe as a whole.
