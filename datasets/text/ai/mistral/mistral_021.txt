The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors:

1. Economic Crisis: France's economy was in dire straits due to heavy government debt accumulated during the Seven Years' War and the American Revolution. The monarchy attempted to address this by imposing new taxes, but these fell disproportionately on the third estate (commoners), who were already struggling with high food prices and unemployment.

2. Political Crisis: The French political system was based on feudalism, where power was divided among the monarchy, the nobility, and the clergy. The third estate, which made up the majority of the population, had no representation in this system. This led to growing discontent and calls for reform.

3. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, particularly those of philosophers like Voltaire, Rousseau, and Montesquieu, influenced many French citizens. These ideas emphasized liberty, equality, and the rule of law, which contrasted sharply with the feudal system and absolute monarchy in France.

4. Bourbon Absolutism: The reigns of Louis XIV, Louis XV, and especially Louis XVI were marked by a centralization of power and a disregard for the needs and concerns of the common people. This led to growing resentment towards the monarchy.

5. Food Shortages and Riots: Frequent food shortages and high prices, particularly during the period known as the Great Fear (1789), led to widespread unrest and rioting in Paris. These events further fueled demands for reform.

6. International Influences: The success of the American Revolution inspired many French revolutionaries, while France's involvement in the War of the Bavarian Succession led to financial strain and public discontent.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm that led to the French Revolution, a period marked by political upheaval, social change, and violence.
