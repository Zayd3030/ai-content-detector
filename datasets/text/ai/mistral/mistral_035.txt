Wind turbines convert the kinetic energy from the wind into mechanical power, which can then be used to generate electricity. Here's a simplified explanation of how they work:

1. **Blade Design**: The wind turbine consists of three main parts: the rotor (with three blades), the nacelle (which houses the generator and other electrical components), and the tower. The blades are designed to capture as much wind energy as possible, with a curved shape that allows them to rotate efficiently.

2. **Rotation**: When the wind blows, it pushes against the rotor blades, causing them to spin around a central hub. This rotation is what generates power. The speed at which the blades spin depends on the wind speed and the size of the turbine.

3. **Conversion of Energy**: Inside the nacelle, a generator converts the mechanical energy from the spinning rotor into electrical energy. As the rotor spins, it turns a shaft connected to the generator, which produces electricity.

4. **Grid Connection**: The generated electricity is then sent through power cables to the electrical grid, where it can be distributed to homes and businesses. Some smaller wind turbines may have batteries for storing energy for later use.

5. **Control Systems**: Modern wind turbines also have sophisticated control systems that adjust the pitch of the blades and the yaw (direction-facing) of the rotor to maximize energy capture based on wind speed and direction. These systems help the turbine operate efficiently in various weather conditions.
