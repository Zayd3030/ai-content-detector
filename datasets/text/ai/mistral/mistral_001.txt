The French Revolution, which took place from 1789 to 1799, was a complex event with multiple underlying causes. Here are some key factors that contributed to its outbreak:

1. Economic Crisis: France's economy was in dire straits due to heavy government debt, agricultural issues, and an outdated feudal system. The burden of taxes fell disproportionately on the common people, while the nobility and clergy were largely exempt. This led to widespread poverty and social unrest.

2. Political Corruption: The French monarchy was plagued by corruption and mismanagement. King Louis XVI and his advisors were seen as out of touch with the needs of the common people, further fueling discontent.

3. Enlightenment Ideas: The ideas of the Enlightenment, particularly those advocating for liberty, equality, and democracy, had a profound impact on French society. These ideas challenged the traditional authority of the monarchy and the Church, and inspired many to demand political reform.

4. Bourgeoisie Ambition: The rising bourgeoisie, or middle class, sought greater political power and economic opportunities. They were frustrated by the restrictions imposed by the feudal system and the monarchy's protection of the privileges of the nobility and clergy.

5. International Factors: France was involved in several costly wars, including the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War, which strained its economy and increased public discontent.

6. Food Shortages and Riots: The period from 1788 to 1789 saw a series of food shortages and price increases, leading to bread riots in Paris. These riots escalated into the Storming of the Bastille on July 14, 1789, which is often considered the beginning of the French Revolution.

These factors combined to create a perfect storm of discontent, ultimately leading to the overthrow of the Bourbon monarchy and the establishment of the First French Republic in 1792.
