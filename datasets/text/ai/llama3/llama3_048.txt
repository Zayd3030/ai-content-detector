Photosynthesis! It's the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy from the sun into chemical energy in the form of glucose (sugar). This vital process is essential for life on Earth, as it provides the energy and organic compounds needed to support the food chain.

Here's a step-by-step explanation of how photosynthesis works:

**Light-Dependent Reactions**

1. **Light absorption**: Light from the sun enters the leaf through tiny openings called stomata.
2. **Chlorophyll**: Chlorophyll, a green pigment found in chloroplasts (organelles within plant cells), absorbs light energy. Chlorophyll is responsible for absorbing blue and red light, but reflects green light, which is why plants appear green to our eyes.
3. **Excitation of electrons**: The absorbed light energy excites electrons in the chlorophyll molecule, leading to an electron transfer.

**Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)**

1. **Carbon fixation**: CO2 from the air enters the leaf through stomata and is fixed into a three-carbon molecule called 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA) via the enzyme RuBisCO.
2. **Reduction reactions**: The energy from light-dependent reactions is used to convert 3-PGA into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), releasing ATP and NADPH in the process.
3. **Regeneration of RuBP**: The enzyme RuBP (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate) is regenerated for the next cycle.

**Overall Equation**

6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 (glucose) + 6 O2

In summary, photosynthesis involves:

* Light-dependent reactions: Light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll, leading to an electron transfer.
* Light-independent reactions (Calvin Cycle): CO2 is fixed into glucose using the energy from light-dependent reactions.

The resulting glucose molecules are used by plants as energy and building blocks for growth and development. Oxygen is released as a byproduct of photosynthesis, which is essential for animal life on Earth.
