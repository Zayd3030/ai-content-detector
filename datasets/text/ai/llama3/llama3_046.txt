The French Revolution was a complex and multifaceted event with various causes that contributed to its outbreak in 1789. Some of the key factors include:

1. **Financial Crisis**: France was deeply in debt from years of war, including the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War. The government's financial troubles led to widespread poverty, unemployment, and economic instability.
2. **Absolute Monarchy**: King Louis XVI ruled France as an absolute monarch, with unchecked power and little accountability. This led to resentment among the common people who felt oppressed by the monarchy.
3. **Social Inequality**: The French society was divided into three estates: the First Estate (clergy), the Second Estate (nobility), and the Third Estate (commoners). The Third Estate, which included peasants, artisans, and merchants, felt marginalized and excluded from power and wealth.
4. **Food Shortages**: A series of poor harvests in the late 18th century led to food shortages, famine, and widespread poverty, particularly among the peasant class.
5. **Influence of Enlightenment Ideas**: The ideas of the Enlightenment, such as liberty, equality, and democracy, had spread throughout France and inspired a desire for change and reform.
6. **American Independence**: The successful American Revolution (1775-1783) inspired French intellectuals and commoners to seek similar independence from their own monarch.
7. **Corruption and Mismanagement**: The French government was plagued by corruption, inefficiency, and mismanagement, which further eroded trust in the monarchy and the aristocracy.
8. **Growing National Debt**: France's national debt had grown significantly due to military expenses and lavish spending by the royal court, leading to increased taxation and economic hardship for the common people.
9. **Feudal System**: The feudal system, which had been abolished in 1780, still maintained many of its privileges and inequalities, contributing to social unrest and discontent among the peasantry.
10. **Cultural and Intellectual Movements**: The French cultural and intellectual movements of the time, such as the rise of literature, art, and science, helped create a sense of national identity and fueled demands for change.

These factors created a perfect storm that ultimately led to the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789, with the famous Tennis Court Oath marking the beginning of the revolution on June 20th.
