Photosynthesis! It's the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy from the sun into chemical energy in the form of glucose (sugar). This process is essential for life on Earth, as it provides the energy and organic compounds needed to support the food chain.

Here's a step-by-step explanation of how photosynthesis works:

**Light-Dependent Reactions**

1. **Light absorption**: Light from the sun enters the leaf through tiny openings called stomata.
2. **Chlorophyll**: Chlorophyll, a green pigment found in chloroplasts (organelles within plant cells), absorbs light energy. Chlorophyll has a unique molecular structure that allows it to absorb blue and red light, but reflect green light, which is why plants appear green.
3. **Excitation of electrons**: When light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll, it excites electrons in the molecule, which are then transferred to a special molecule called an electron acceptor.

**Light-Independent Reactions (Calvin Cycle)**

1. **Carbon fixation**: The excited electrons from the light-dependent reactions are used to convert carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O) into glucose (C6H12O6) and oxygen (O2).
2. **Reduction of CO2**: CO2 is fixed into a three-carbon molecule called 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA) through the enzyme RuBisCO.
3. **Regeneration of RuBP**: The enzyme RuBP (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate) is regenerated from 3-PGA and CO2.
4. **Production of glucose**: Glucose is produced through a series of reactions involving the conversion of 3-PGA into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P).
5. **Oxygen production**: Oxygen is released as a byproduct of photosynthesis.

**Overall Equation**

6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 (glucose) + 6O2

In summary, photosynthesis involves the absorption of light energy by chlorophyll, which excites electrons that are then used to convert CO2 and H2O into glucose and O2. This process is essential for plant growth and development, as well as supporting life on Earth through the food chain.
