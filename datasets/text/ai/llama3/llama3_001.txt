The French Revolution was a complex and multifaceted event with various causes that contributed to its outbreak in 1789. Some of the key factors include:

1. **Financial Crisis**: France was deeply in debt from years of war, including the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War. The government's financial troubles led to widespread poverty, unemployment, and economic instability.
2. **Absolute Monarchy**: King Louis XVI ruled France as an absolute monarch, with unchecked power and no constitutional limits. This led to resentment among the population, who felt that their voices were not being heard.
3. **Social Inequality**: The French society was divided into three estates: the First Estate (clergy), the Second Estate (nobility), and the Third Estate (commoners). The common people felt oppressed by the privileged classes and sought greater social mobility and equality.
4. **Food Shortages and Famine**: A series of poor harvests in the late 18th century led to food shortages, famine, and widespread poverty, particularly among the peasants and urban working class.
5. **Inefficient Taxation System**: The French tax system was unfair and inefficient, with the burden falling disproportionately on the common people. This led to widespread resentment and calls for reform.
6. **Enlightenment Ideas**: The Enlightenment had spread throughout Europe, including France, and had inspired ideas about liberty, equality, and democracy. These ideas resonated with many ordinary French citizens who felt that their government was not serving their interests.
7. **American Independence**: The American Revolution's success in 1783 had shown that a people could overthrow their monarch and establish a republic. This inspired similar aspirations among the French people.
8. **Corruption and Mismanagement**: The French monarchy and aristocracy were seen as corrupt and incompetent, with many officials enriching themselves at the expense of the common people.
9. **Growing Nationalism**: As the French economy stagnated, there was growing resentment towards foreign powers, particularly Austria and Prussia, which were perceived as threats to France's independence and prosperity.

These factors created a perfect storm that ultimately led to the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789. The Estates-General, a representative assembly, was convened to address the financial crisis, but it soon became clear that the people wanted more than just economic reforms – they sought fundamental changes to the social and political order.
