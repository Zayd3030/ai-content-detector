The French Revolution, which lasted from 1789 to 1799, was a complex and multifaceted event with various causes that contributed to its outbreak. Some of the key factors include:

1. **Financial Crisis**: France was deeply in debt due to costly wars and lavish spending by King Louis XVI and his wife, Marie Antoinette. This financial strain led to widespread poverty, unemployment, and economic hardship.
2. **Social Inequality**: The French society was divided into three estates: the First Estate (clergy), the Second Estate (nobility), and the Third Estate (commoners). The Third Estate, which included peasants, artisans, and merchants, felt oppressed by the privileges of the nobility and clergy.
3. **Absolute Monarchy**: King Louis XVI ruled France as an absolute monarch, with little accountability to his subjects. This led to feelings of resentment and frustration among the common people.
4. **Enlightenment Ideas**: The Enlightenment, a philosophical movement that emphasized reason, individual rights, and democracy, had gained popularity in France. This intellectual ferment helped create a sense of discontent with the existing social order.
5. **Agricultural Crisis**: A series of poor harvests and crop failures led to food shortages and high prices, exacerbating economic woes and social tensions.
6. **National Debt**: The French government was heavily indebted from its involvement in the Seven Years' War (1756-1763) and the American Revolutionary War (1775-1783). This debt burden contributed to the financial crisis.
7. **Corruption and Mismanagement**: The French monarchy and aristocracy were seen as corrupt and incompetent, with many officials enriching themselves at the expense of the common people.
8. **Growing Nationalism**: As the American Revolution gained momentum, French intellectuals and patriots began to question their own country's monarchical system and seek a more democratic form of government.
9. **The Estates-General**: In 1789, King Louis XVI called the Estates-General, a representative assembly that had not met since 1614. The Third Estate, which included commoners and representatives of the middle class, demanded greater representation and reform, leading to the outbreak of the French Revolution.

These factors created a perfect storm of discontent, leading to the French Revolution's outbreak in May 1789. The revolutionaries sought to overthrow the monarchy, establish a more equal society, and create a new form of government based on liberty, equality, and fraternity.
