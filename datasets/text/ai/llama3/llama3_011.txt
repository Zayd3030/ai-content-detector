The French Revolution was a complex and multifaceted event with various causes that contributed to its outbreak in 1789. Some of the key factors include:

1. **Financial Crisis**: France was deeply in debt from years of war, including the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War. The government was struggling to pay its debts, which led to a financial crisis.
2. **Absolute Monarchy**: King Louis XVI ruled France as an absolute monarch, with unchecked power and little accountability. This led to widespread discontent among the population.
3. **Social Inequality**: The French society was divided into three estates: the First Estate (clergy), the Second Estate (nobility), and the Third Estate (commoners). The Third Estate, which included peasants, artisans, and merchants, felt oppressed by the privileges of the other two estates.
4. **Economic Hardship**: Many people were struggling to make ends meet due to poverty, famine, and high prices for basic goods like bread. The population was growing faster than the food supply, leading to widespread hunger and malnutrition.
5. **Ideas of Enlightenment**: The ideas of the Enlightenment, such as liberty, equality, and fraternity, had spread throughout France, inspiring people to demand greater individual rights and freedoms.
6. **American Revolution**: The success of the American Revolution in 1783 inspired French intellectuals and commoners to seek similar reforms in their own country.
7. **Corruption and Mismanagement**: The government was seen as corrupt and inefficient, with a bloated bureaucracy and a lack of effective governance.
8. **Food Shortages**: A series of poor harvests and crop failures led to food shortages, which exacerbated social tensions and economic hardship.
9. **National Debt**: France's national debt had grown significantly due to the wars and lavish spending by the monarchy. This created pressure on the government to find ways to reduce its debt.
10. **Growing Unrest**: As these factors combined, a growing sense of unrest and discontent spread throughout France, particularly among the common people and intellectuals.

These causes created a perfect storm that ultimately led to the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789, with the famous Tennis Court Oath on June 20 marking a turning point in the revolution's early stages.
