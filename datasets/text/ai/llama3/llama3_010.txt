Wind turbines are amazing machines that generate electricity from the wind. Here's how they work in simple terms:

**Main Parts:**

1. **Rotor Blades:** Long, thin blades that spin around when the wind blows.
2. **Hub:** The central part of the turbine where the rotor blades attach.
3. **Generator:** Converts the spinning motion into electrical energy.

**How it Works:**

1. **Wind Blows:** When the wind blows, it makes the rotor blades spin around the hub.
2. **Energy Generation:** As the blades spin, they create a force that drives the generator to produce electricity.
3. **Electricity Output:** The generated electricity is sent through a transformer and then fed into the power grid.

**Key Points:**

* Wind turbines work best when the wind blows steadily at speeds between 5-25 miles per hour (8-40 km/h).
* As the blades spin, they create a force called torque that drives the generator.
* The direction of the wind determines which way the turbine spins. Some turbines can adjust their angle to optimize energy production.

**In Simple Terms:** Wind turbines use spinning blades to capture the kinetic energy in the wind and convert it into electrical energy. It's like using a giant fan to generate power!
