The French Revolution was a complex and multifaceted event with various causes that contributed to its outbreak in 1789. Some of the key factors include:

1. **Financial Crisis**: France was deeply in debt from years of war, including the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War. The government was struggling to pay its debts, which led to a financial crisis.
2. **Absolute Monarchy**: King Louis XVI ruled France as an absolute monarch, with unchecked power and no constitutional limits. This led to widespread discontent among the population.
3. **Social Inequality**: The French society was divided into three estates: the First Estate (clergy), the Second Estate (nobility), and the Third Estate (commoners). The commoners were largely poor and lacked representation in government, while the nobility and clergy held most of the power and wealth.
4. **Food Shortages and Poverty**: A series of bad harvests and economic downturns had led to widespread poverty and food shortages, particularly among the peasants and urban working class.
5. **Influence of Enlightenment Ideas**: The ideas of the Enlightenment, such as liberty, equality, and democracy, were spreading throughout France and inspiring calls for reform and revolution.
6. **American Independence**: The successful American Revolution (1775-1783) had inspired many in France to seek similar independence from their own monarchies.
7. **Corruption and Mismanagement**: The French government was seen as corrupt and inefficient, with a bloated bureaucracy and a lack of effective governance.
8. **Growing National Debt**: The cost of the American Revolutionary War and other military campaigns had increased the national debt, which further strained the already fragile economy.
9. **Tensions between the King and the Nobility**: There was growing tension between King Louis XVI and the nobility, who felt that he was not doing enough to address their concerns and protect their interests.
10. **The Role of the Estates-General**: The Estates-General, a representative assembly of the three estates, had not met since 1614. In May 1789, it was convened again, but the representatives were divided along estate lines, leading to stalemate and frustration.

These factors created a perfect storm that ultimately led to the outbreak of the French Revolution in July 1789, with the famous Tennis Court Oath marking the beginning of the revolution.
