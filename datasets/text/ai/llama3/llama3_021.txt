The French Revolution, which lasted from 1789 to 1799, was a complex and multifaceted event with various causes that contributed to its outbreak. Some of the key factors include:

1. **Financial Crisis**: France was deeply in debt due to costly wars and lavish spending by King Louis XVI and his wife, Marie Antoinette. This financial strain led to widespread poverty, unemployment, and economic hardship.
2. **Social Inequality**: The French society was divided into three estates: the First Estate (clergy), the Second Estate (nobility), and the Third Estate (commoners). The common people felt oppressed by the privileged classes, who held most of the power and wealth.
3. **Absolute Monarchy**: King Louis XVI ruled France as an absolute monarch, with little accountability to his subjects. This led to feelings of resentment and frustration among the population.
4. **Enlightenment Ideas**: The Enlightenment's emphasis on reason, individual rights, and democracy had spread throughout Europe, including France. This intellectual movement inspired many to question the authority of the monarchy and seek reforms.
5. **Agricultural Crisis**: A series of poor harvests in the late 18th century led to food shortages and high prices, exacerbating social tensions and economic hardship.
6. **National Debt**: The French government had accumulated a significant national debt from its involvement in the Seven Years' War and the American Revolutionary War. This debt was largely owed to wealthy creditors who demanded repayment.
7. **Corruption and Mismanagement**: The French monarchy was plagued by corruption, nepotism, and mismanagement, which further eroded trust among the population.
8. **Growing Middle Class**: A growing middle class of merchants, artisans, and professionals emerged in France during the 18th century. This new social group sought greater economic opportunities and political representation.
9. **Influence of the American Revolution**: The successful American Revolution (1775-1783) inspired many French intellectuals and revolutionaries to seek similar reforms in their own country.

These factors created a perfect storm that ultimately led to the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789, with the famous Tennis Court Oath marking the beginning of the event.
